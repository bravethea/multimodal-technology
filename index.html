Computational analysis of multimodal human communication

People convey meaning through speech, language, facial expression, gesture and posture at the same time, yet these signals are usually recorded and analysed in isolation. The Multimodal Technology Group develops methods, datasets and research infrastructure for analysing them jointly, and studies what their combination reveals about identity, interaction and linguistic variation.

The group is based at the Department of Computational Linguistics, University of Zurich. Its projects are carried out with the Digital Society Initiative (DSI) and the Linguistic Research Infrastructure (LiRI), and in collaboration with partners in Switzerland and abroad.

Established 2023 Group leader: Dr. Teodora Vuković Zurich, Switzerland

Research areas

Four lines of work, described in detail on the research page.

Multimodal person recognition and identity

Models that recognise and characterise individuals from the combination of voice, face and conversational dynamics, with emphasis on interpretability, robustness to missing modalities, and the ethical implications of identity inference.

CAPIRE · TidyVoice · NIST SRE

Infrastructure for audio-visual and multimodal data

Platforms and annotation tools that make large collections of video, audio and transcribed speech searchable and analysable, and that allow infrastructures to exchange multimodal data in interoperable formats.

LiRI Corpus Platform · DAVA Lab · MAVA Cluster

Behaviour, physiology and subjective experience

Methods for relating observable behavioural signals to physiological measurements and to participants' own reports of their experience, and for processing these data types within a single analytical workflow.

MOSAIC

Spoken and low-resource language resources

Corpora, annotation methods and processing tools for spoken varieties with few existing resources, including the Torlak dialect of the central Balkans, and the methodological questions that such data raise.

Spoken Torlak corpus · Part-of-speech tagging

News

  • September 2026

    The TidyVoice 2026 Challenge evaluation plan was presented at Interspeech 2026, as part of a collaboration on speech data quality with partners at Idiap, Mozilla Common Voice, Indiana University and Otto von Guericke University Magdeburg.

  • 2026

    The chapter Spoken Corpora appeared in the third edition of the International Encyclopedia of Language and Linguistics (Elsevier).

  • February 2026

    The group organised the workshop Infrastructure for Multimodal Audio-Visual and Bio-signal Human Data at the University of Zurich.

  • February 2026

    MOSAIC started, funded by the Innosuisse Innovation Booster Artificial Intelligence. The project develops a data science platform for the integrated processing of physiological, behavioural, linguistic, clinical and self-reported data.

  • November 2025

    The group organised the conference Frontiers and Ethics of Multimodal AI Technology at the Digital Society Initiative, University of Zurich.

  • October 2025

    Masoumeh Chapariniya's paper Beyond Appearance: Transformer-based Person Identification from Conversational Dynamics received the second best paper award at ICCKE 2025.

  • September 2025

    The DAVA Lab (Digital Audio-Visual Annotation Lab) started, funded by a DSI Infrastructure & Labs grant, to develop software for automatic audio-visual and multimodal data analysis.

  • August 2025

    The LiRI Corpus Platform was demonstrated at Interspeech 2025 in Rotterdam.

  • July 2025

    The MAVA Cluster began work on interoperability between multimodal and audio-visual research infrastructures, with the Swiss Data Science Center (ETH Zurich), LiRI and the TIB AV-Portal in Hannover.
