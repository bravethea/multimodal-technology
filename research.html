Research

The group works on the joint analysis of speech, language, facial expression, body movement and physiological signals. The projects below combine method development, infrastructure work and empirical studies; most are carried out with partners inside and outside the University of Zurich.

Research topics

  • Multimodal person recognition

    Recognition and verification of individuals from voice, face and movement. Current questions concern fusion strategies for heterogeneous modalities, behaviour under missing or degraded modalities, and language-independent face–voice association.

  • Identity beyond appearance

    Whether and how individuals can be characterised from conversational dynamics — timing, turn-taking, gesture and posture — rather than from static appearance, and what this implies for privacy and for the interpretation of such models.

  • Multimodal corpus representation

    Data models and query methods for corpora that combine transcribed speech, video, annotation layers and time-aligned measurements, and the theoretical questions raised by representing several dimensions of the same recording.

  • Integration of behavioural, physiological and self-report data

    Analytical workflows that treat observable behaviour, physiological measurement and participants' own accounts of their experience as parts of one dataset rather than separate studies.

  • Spoken and low-resource language processing

    Transcription, annotation and part-of-speech tagging for spoken varieties with little existing data, and the documentation of vulnerable varieties such as Torlak.

  • Ethics of multimodal analysis

    The conditions under which inference about persons from audio-visual data is defensible: consent, interpretability, the limits of what such models support, and the governance of multimodal datasets.

Current projects

CAPIRE

Computational Analysis of Personal Identity in Interaction: Recognition and Ethics. Explainable machine learning for patterns of personal identity in speech, facial expression and gesture, together with an analysis of the ethical questions that identity recognition raises.

2023–2027 · DSI Bridge Postdoc Grant, Digital Society Initiative · with the Department of Computational Linguistics

DAVA Lab

Digital Audio-Visual Annotation Lab. Development of software for the automatic annotation and analysis of audio-visual and multimodal data, intended for use in academic research and in industry.

2025–2027 · DSI Infrastructure & Labs Grant · with LiRI

MAVA Cluster

Multimodal and Audio-Visual Analysis Cluster for Infrastructure Interoperability and Data Exchange. Shared formats and exchange mechanisms that allow audio-visual research infrastructures to interoperate.

2025–2027 · Collaborative Open Research Data Infrastructure Projects, Swiss Data Science Center · with SDSC (ETH Zurich), LiRI and TIB AV-Portal (Hannover)

MOSAIC

Multimodal Open-Science AI-Powered Integrative Computation. A data science platform for processing human signals — physiological, behavioural, linguistic, clinical and self-reported — individually or simultaneously.

2026– · Innosuisse Innovation Booster Artificial Intelligence

LaDaD

Linguistic Data Across Disciplines. Teaching materials and workshops on data analytics and computational methods for language data, addressed to researchers in disciplines that work with language but not primarily with computational methods.

2026 · swissuniversities Open Research Data call · with CLARIN-CH and LiRI

LiRI Corpus Platform

A web-based infrastructure for building, querying and analysing multimodal corpora, combining transcribed speech, video and annotation layers in a single query environment.

2021– · UZH TPF grant · co-led with Prof. Dr. Noah Bubenhofer · see Resources

Earlier work

  • VIAN-DH

    until 2023

    Software for multimodal video analysis in the digital humanities, since integrated into the LiRI Corpus Platform.

  • Spoken Torlak corpus

    2015–2026

    Fieldwork, transcription and annotation of the Timok variety of Torlak, resulting in a published corpus, a part-of-speech tagger and corpus-based studies of grammatical variation. A second version of the corpus and tagger is in preparation.

  • TraCeBa

    2017–2021

    (Dis-)entangling Traditions on the Central Balkans: Performance and Perception. Creation of electronic language resources and corpus-based analysis of linguistic variation, funded by ERA.Net RUS Plus.
